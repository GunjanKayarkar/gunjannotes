Shooting in 4:3 will ensure more sharpness, higher megapixels and if you just absolutely want the photo to be in 16:9, then you can just crop the better quality 4:3 photo and its 16:9 version will just be equal to a normally taken 16:9 photo. For most photos, 4:3 is great, but since 16:9 is a bit wider, so it helps when clicking panoramas 





F - Focus 

S - Shutter Priority/Speed 

ISO - International Organization for Standardization 

WB - White Balance 





F deals with the focus aspect, what to focus on, what will be shown in high quality in the image 





S deals with how long the shutter of the camera stays open. It is usually measured in seconds, ranging from 1/1000 seconds to 32 seconds. Less shutter speed is good for taking quick snaps of moving objects, essentially sports mode. While large shutter speed, which usually renders the photo a bit blur, is useful if you want to click, lets say the photo of a scenery, then you can use a longer shutter speed so that the output photo is a bit smoothened out, it will kind of look more blurry smooth, especially the sky and grass, etc 





ISO refers to how sensitive the image sensor is to light. It essentially deals with how bright the photo will be. Higher the ISO, brighter is the image, so this can be used in Low light settings. Also, this aspect usually needs to be coupled with Shutter Speed (S) to get appropriate results, otherwise it is of no use. If the S and ISO are both very high, then the image also comes off as extra bright, almost white. Also, a higher ISO means that the sharpness of the image will decrease and the image at the highest ISO is generally grainy to look at, not offering much detail 





WB essentially deals with how the colors of the photo look, if the WB is very low, the photo looks bluish, while if the WB is very high, the photo looks reddish. Typically, 5,500K to 5,700K is considered to offer a neutral colour tone

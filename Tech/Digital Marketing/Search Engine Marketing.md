# **Search Engine Marketing** 
A) It is those results that are displayed as ads when something is searched. For example when searching cloud storage, if dropbox is at the very top and onedrive is somewhere in the middle, then high probability that dropbox is a result of SEM and has been paid for while Onedrive is SEO and organic. The advertisers pay/bid for different words and have to shell out the bid money per word clicked i.e. if the searched word has redirected 20 people bcoz of advertised results, and highest bid was of 10$, then advertiser needs to shell put 200$, but if redirected traffic is 0, then no money is required. But then the thought process of bidding on word varies, for example memers would have bid more for "noice' and "420" while others would have not. 









B) Also, even if you have paid 2$ pay-per-click for "Green Apples" while another rival has paid 1$ PPC for "Green Apples Nagpur", but if the person searches for green apples nagpur then the 2nd one will go higher, but otherwise normally, the higher bid will rank higher. As you can see, you can’t simply “buy your way to the top.” This is to ensure the most relevant adverts win the auction, not simply the ones with the highest bids. If your ads lack relevance and get a low Quality Score, it’ll be difficult to compete. In fact, if relevance is too low, the search engines may not display your ads at all. 





C) NEGATIVE KEYWORDS: 

Imagine the case where you are a photographer using SEM to get people to visit your page, but what if there are also many who are searching for watercolour pet portraits, and horse portraits, neither of which you offer. In situations like these, you should use “negative keywords” to prevent your ads from appearing when people search for things that aren’t relevant to your business. For example, [-pencil], [-pastel] and [-horse]. 





These negative keywords will block your ads any time a search contains one or all of them. 





This is useful, because preventing adverts from appearing for irrelevant searches, is going to save you money, and make sure that only your ideal customer sees your ads. 





Because just think what if people searching for wedding drawing just click on your ads, visit your page but do not take note of your page or are interested in your business but wanted drawings, so now since they clicked on your ad, you will have to pay for the click while getting no potential customer out of the click 









D) On top of that, you can add match type to further refine the results. How do you do that? Let’s take a look. Keywords are broad match by default. Aside from broad match, the other primary match types are phrase match and exact match. To change broad match to phrase, simply place quotations around the keyword. So, the keyword London portrait photographer becomes “London portrait photographer.” 





Phrase match tells Google Ads or Bing Ads that adverts can’t be displayed unless the search includes the entire phrase. So if someone searches for “London portrait photographer” that’s great-your ads can show up! Minor variations, like plurals, are included. This means that a search for “London portrait photographers” can also trigger your ad. 





But, if someone searches for the more generic London photographer, your ad won’t show up, because the word portrait is missing.
